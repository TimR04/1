> *(A current, supportive letter of recommendation from a university teacher, providing information about your qualification.)*

### 1.6 Confidentiality

Three distinct findings, which must not be conflated:

1. **In practice, the applicant sees the letter.** Because the applicant uploads it (1.4), the letter passes through the applicant's hands as a PDF. There is no mechanism in this programme by which the referee submits confidentially and directly. **Classification: B** (strong inference from the verified upload mechanism).
2. **DAAD treats submitted Gutachten confidentially on its side**, does not return them to the applicant, and may forward them within one year if requested. `[via search extraction]` Source: https://www.daad.de/de/im-ausland-studieren-forschen-lehren/stipendien-finanzierung/wichtige-hinweise-zu-daad-stipendien/ — **Tier 1 — Classification A (indirect)**
3. **A legacy postal/sealed-envelope route exists in older DAAD material** (see Section 10, Contradiction C1). Not applicable to this programme per the current page.

**Practical consequence:** the referee cannot rely on the letter being invisible to the applicant. A referee who wants genuine confidentiality has no route here; conversely, the applicant should not promise the professor that the letter will be unread by them, because that is not true.

### 3.6 Academic ranking / comparison with peers ("top 5%")

**This is a genuine feature of German Gutachten culture, not an Anglo import.**

- The **Studienstiftung des deutschen Volkes** — the reference point for German academic nomination culture — requires proposing academics to judge whether the student belongs to **the top ~10% of their cohort**, and asks them to **classify the grade relative to cohort size / place the candidate in a comparison group**.
Sources: https://www.studienstiftung.de/infos-fuer-studierende-und-vorschlagende/hochschullehrende ; https://www.studienstiftung.de/pool/sdv/public/documents/auswahl/dokumente/Leitfragen_f%C3%BCr_das_Vorschlagsgutachten_11-2024.pdf — **Tier 2 — Classification C (strong, multiple German institutional sources)**
- DAAD itself: **no source found that requires a percentile statement.** **NOT VERIFIED for DAAD. Classification C for "expected in German Gutachten culture"; D for "DAAD asks for it".**

**Recommendation (C):** include a **verifiable, bounded** cohort statement, e.g. *"unter den besten X von Y Studierenden des Jahrgangs, die ich in [Modul] unterrichtet habe"* or *"die beste Bachelorarbeit, die ich in den letzten N Jahren betreut habe"*. **Bounded and countable beats an unbounded "top 1% worldwide".** It is exactly the information a committee cannot get from the transcript, and it survives German scepticism about superlatives because it is falsifiable.

> **Caveat specific to this applicant:** a DHBW cohort is small and the reference group is unfamiliar to a DAAD committee. The referee should therefore state **the size and nature of the comparison group** ("von 28 Studierenden des Kurses IMBIT-Jahrgang 20xx"), otherwise the percentile is uninterpretable. **B.**

### 3.7 Importance of concrete examples

**Highest-value element of the letter. Classification C (very strong, convergent across German and Anglo sources).**

- Weak letters are those that are *generic, share no anecdotes, and list facts that could describe any student with a similar CV* — described as the recogniser turning CV bullet points into prose. https://blog.prepscholar.com/what-makes-for-a-bad-college-recommendation
- Letters that *clarify how the referee knows the candidate and describe the experiential basis of positive evaluative judgements* are valued more. https://topscholars.oregonstate.edu/faculty-and-staff/writing-effective-recommendations
- German guidance: the Gutachter should state **where, how long and how well** they know the candidate before assessing them; the letter should be *"so konkret und individuell wie möglich"* rather than empty phrases. https://www.e-fellows.net/stipendien/gutachten-selbst-schreiben ; https://www.mystipendium.de/bewerbung-stipendium/empfehlungsschreiben-stipendium
- Uni Trier states the principle sharply: *"Ein solches Zeugnis kann kein bloßes Gefälligkeitsattest sein; die zu bescheinigende besondere Qualifikation muss nachweisbar sein."* — a reference cannot be a mere courtesy attestation; the special qualification certified must be demonstrable. https://www.uni-trier.de/universitaet/fachbereiche-faecher/fachbereich-v/personen/professuren/prof-dr-diederich-eckardt/forschung/hinweise-zu-gutachten-und-empfehlungsschreiben — **Tier 2 — C**

**Rule of thumb (C): every evaluative adjective in the letter should be followed by an episode that licenses it.** For this applicant the richest seam is the **Bachelor's thesis on AI / claim verification** — problem chosen, method, independence, how the applicant handled setbacks, quality of the result, grade in context.

### 3.8 Should the referee discuss extracurricular engagement?

**Yes — but only what they can personally vouch for. Classification B (strong inference) / A (indirect) on the criterion.**

DAAD explicitly lists *"außerfachliche Kenntnisse und Fähigkeiten sowie gesellschaftliches Engagement"* among its selection criteria (1.9). A referee who confirms it adds independent corroboration to what is otherwise self-reported in the CV.

**But:** a professor who never saw the applicant's Vereinsarbeit should not narrate it — that is precisely the kind of CV-restating that weakens letters (3.7). **Correct move (B): one sentence at most, framed as what the referee observed** — e.g. that the applicant combined a demanding practice-integrated degree and a thesis with sustained voluntary commitments, and what that implied for their self-organisation *as the referee experienced it* (deadlines met, work delivered while carrying other loads). The *detail* of the engagement belongs in the CV and motivation letter, not the Gutachten.

### 3.9 Should career potential be discussed?

**Yes. Classification B (strong) — DAAD's criteria include "Motivation und Perspektiven", i.e. what the stay abroad means for further development; and the DAAD recommendation forms in adjacent programmes explicitly ask what significance the scholarship has for the applicant's scientific and professional development.** (Source for the latter: https://www2.daad.de/medien/deutschland/stipendien/formulare/gutachten_ungarn.pdf — different programme, hence supporting only, **D** for direct transfer.)

**What it should look like (B):** a short, sober forecast tied to evidence — what the referee expects the applicant to be able to do after the MPhil, and why the referee believes it given what they observed. Avoid unfalsifiable prophecy ("will be a leader of his generation"). Prefer: *"I expect him to work at the interface of AI methods and industrial operations; the thesis showed he can take a research method from the literature and make it work on a real problem, which is what that interface requires."*

### 3.10 Should the specific Cambridge programme be addressed?

**Yes — and this is where most letters lose points. Classification B (strong).**

DAAD assesses the **quality of the project** (study plan) as a distinct criterion (1.9). The referee is uniquely positioned to say something the applicant cannot say about themselves: **that the chosen programme is the right one and the applicant can complete it.**

Three things the referee can credibly say about fit (**B**):
1. **Substantive fit** — why an MPhil in Industrial Systems, Manufacture and Management at the IfM follows logically from a Wirtschaftsinformatik/IMBIT degree plus an AI thesis, i.e. the applicant already sits on the IT/business boundary that ISMM addresses.
2. **Capacity to cope** — that the applicant has the quantitative and English-language capability for an 11-month, intensive, industry-facing Cambridge MPhil, evidenced by coursework and the thesis.
3. **Why abroad, why Cambridge, why now** — that this specific training is not equivalently available in Germany, and that the applicant's stated plans are realistic rather than aspirational.

> **Prevention: the programme must be named exactly and correctly** — *MPhil in Industrial Systems, Manufacture and Management, Institute for Manufacturing / Department of Engineering, University of Cambridge*. Naming the wrong course or the wrong university is a well-known failure mode (6.6). Give the referee the exact string.
> Programme page: https://www.ifm.eng.cam.ac.uk/education/industrial-systems-manufacture-management-course/ ; https://www.postgraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/courses/directory/egegmpimm (Tier 1)

### 3.11 How strongly should the recommender endorse? (see full analysis in Section 5)

**Strongly and unambiguously — in a German register. Classification C.**

DAAD's own word for the required letter is **"unterstützend"** (supportive) — a lukewarm letter literally fails the stated requirement (**A (indirect)**). But the endorsement must be carried by **evidence and cohort placement**, not by adjective stacking. See Section 5.

### 3.12 The Cambridge reference is a SEPARATE, different animal

This matters operationally and is frequently confused.

| | **DAAD Gutachten** | **Cambridge academic reference** |
|---|---|---|
| Number | **1** | **2** academic referees (3 if applying for Gates Cambridge) |
| Who submits | **The applicant uploads** | **The referee submits directly** via the Referee Portal (separate from the Applicant Portal); may upload a file or type into a free-text box |
| Confidential to applicant? | No (applicant handles the file) | **Yes — "References will not be disclosed to the student or to third parties, including other parts of the University of Cambridge."** |
| Deadline | DAAD application deadline | **The Cambridge course deadline; a reference uploaded after the deadline cannot be accepted, and without both references the application is not considered by the department or funding bodies** |
| Form | DAAD Deckblatt + free letter | Cambridge's own reference request; no DAAD cover sheet |
| Timing note | — | Up to 36 hours for a submitted reference to register; the application may be submitted before references arrive but is not complete until they do |

Sources (**Tier 1**): https://www.postgraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/apply/how/references ; https://www.postgraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/apply/how/academic-referees ; https://www.postgraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/apply/how/supporting-documents ; https://camadmissions.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/22649692042770-Applicant-Portal-Help-Reference-request ; https://camadmissions.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/8851124352914 — **Classification A (indirect — search extraction)**

**Implications (B):**
- The professor will likely be asked **twice**: once for DAAD (letter handed back to the applicant, plus the DAAD Deckblatt) and once for Cambridge (submitted directly by the professor through the Cambridge Referee Portal, confidential). **Tell them this up front**, so they budget for it and so the Cambridge request does not arrive as a surprise from an unknown noreply address.
- **Cambridge needs a second academic referee.** Plan that now, not later. The DAAD-eligible writer is one; the second Cambridge referee is a separate choice.
- The Cambridge reference should be written in the **Anglo register** (Section 5); the DAAD one in the German register. They are not the same document and should not be a copy-paste.
- Cambridge does not require the DAAD cover sheet, and DAAD does not accept a Cambridge-portal submission. **Do not cross the wires.**

## 4. Ideal structure of the letter (section by section)

Target: **one dense page (≈500–650 words), max two.** German business-letter form, institutional letterhead, dated, signed, stamped if available. Language: German or English — **English is defensible here and arguably better, since the study destination is anglophone; no DAAD language requirement for the Gutachten was found (NOT VERIFIED — check the portal form, which may state a language).** Classification of the whole section: **C**, built on 1.9 (A-indirect) + convergent German and Anglo writing guidance.

| # | Section | Purpose | Content | Evidence the referee needs | Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Heading / Betreff** | Instant identification | *"Empfehlungsschreiben für Herrn [Name] — Bewerbung um ein DAAD-Stipendium für ein Masterstudium im Ausland (MPhil in Industrial Systems, Manufacture and Management, University of Cambridge, Studienbeginn Oktober 2027)"* | Exact programme and university string | 2 lines |
| 2 | **Basis of knowledge** | Establishes standing; the single most common omission | Where, how long, in what role the referee knows the applicant; which modules, which supervision, over what period; the size of the group in which they observed them | Module names, semester, cohort size, supervision period | 2–4 sentences |
| 3 | **Academic qualification — the core** | Feeds DAAD criterion 1 | What the applicant actually did: the thesis (topic, method, result, grade), performance in the referee's own teaching, quantitative capability, independence, quality of scientific work | **One concrete episode, told in detail.** For this applicant: the AI / claim-verification thesis | ~35–40% of the letter |
| 4 | **Cohort placement** | Gives the committee calibration it cannot get from a transcript | A bounded, countable comparison: rank within a named group of stated size, or "best thesis I have supervised in N years" | Real numbers the referee can defend | 1–2 sentences |
| 5 | **Personal suitability / working style** | Feeds "persönliche Eignung", motivation | Reliability, initiative, intellectual curiosity, how the applicant handled difficulty, ability to work across disciplines and in English | An episode, not adjectives | 2–4 sentences |
| 6 | **Non-academic / civic engagement** | Feeds DAAD criterion on gesellschaftliches Engagement | **Only what the referee observed**: that substantial voluntary responsibility ran alongside a demanding dual degree, and what that says about self-organisation | First-hand observation only | 1–2 sentences |
| 7 | **Fit with the specific programme and plan** | Feeds "Qualität des Vorhabens" | Why ISMM at the IfM is the right next step; why the applicant can complete it; why it is not equivalently available in Germany | Referee must have read the study plan | 3–5 sentences |
| 8 | **Career potential** | Feeds "Perspektiven" | Sober forecast anchored in observed capability; what the scholarship makes possible that would otherwise not be | Reasoning, not prophecy | 2–3 sentences |
| 9 | **Explicit endorsement** | DAAD requires a *supportive* letter | An unambiguous, unhedged sentence of support for the award of the scholarship — no conditionals, no "I would say that…" | — | 1–2 sentences |
| 10 | **Availability / signature block** | Formality and credibility | Offer of further information, full name, title, chair/programme, institution, contact details, date, signature (+ stamp) | — | 4–6 lines |

**What to leave out:** a chronological CV recap; grades already on the transcript without interpretation; anything the referee did not witness; hedges and caveats that the referee does not actually mean.

## 5. German understatement vs Anglo endorsement: analysis and recommendation

### 5.1 The tension is real and documented

- **Anglo-American norm:** the letter is an advocacy document; it must be *"entirely and energetically and overtly positive"*; the absence of superlatives is itself read as a negative signal; weaknesses are not mentioned. https://theprofessorisin.com/2016/09/07/how-to-write-a-recommendation-letter/ ; https://dailynous.com/2016/12/15/letters-recommendation/ — **Tier 3 — C**
- **German norm:** unmodulated superlatives read as insincere or inflated; German academic assessment culture treats raising qualifications and criticisms as part of the reviewer's job; German reference documents are **shorter and more factual** than American ones. https://theprofessorisin.com/2016/09/07/how-to-write-a-recommendation-letter/ ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0889490697000124 (Precht, cross-cultural comparison of letters of recommendation) — **Tier 3 / academic literature — C**
- **The failure mode runs in both directions:** a German-register letter read by an Anglo committee reads as tepid; an Anglo-register letter read by a German committee reads as unserious. **C.**

### 5.2 Who actually reads the DAAD Gutachten

DAAD selection committees are composed *"überwiegend [aus] Professorinnen und Professoren sowie Lehrende deutscher Hochschulen"* — predominantly professors and lecturers at **German** universities.
Source: https://www.daad.de/de/im-ausland-studieren-forschen-lehren/stipendien-finanzierung/wichtige-hinweise-zu-daad-stipendien/ `[via search extraction]` — **Tier 1 — A (indirect)**

**This settles the register question: the DAAD Gutachten is read by German academics and must be written in the German register.** The Cambridge reference is read by a Cambridge department and should be written in the Anglo register. **Do not use one text for both.** **Classification B (inference from an A-indirect fact).**

### 5.3 Recommendation

**Write the DAAD Gutachten as "German form, unambiguous substance".**

| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Unambiguous **endorsement in one clean sentence** at the end — DAAD's own requirement is a *"unterstützendes"* letter (**A indirect**) | A conclusion that only says the applicant "is suitable" / "erfüllt die Voraussetzungen" |
| **Calibration by counting**: "best of 28", "among the three strongest theses I have supervised since 20xx" | "one of the very best students in the world", "top 1%" without a reference group |
| **Evidence-first**: episode → judgement | Adjective chains ("outstanding, exceptional, brilliant") with nothing attached |
| Plain German academic register, factual tone | Emotive Anglo idiom translated literally into German ("I cannot recommend him highly enough") |
| **No hedges the referee does not mean.** German understatement is a *stylistic* register, not a licence to qualify. A sentence like *"gehört zu den besten Studierenden, die ich unterrichtet habe"* is a strong claim in German and should be stated flatly, without "meines Erachtens vielleicht" | Ritual German modesty markers ("relativ gut", "durchaus solide", "im Großen und Ganzen") — these are *read as criticism* by German committees |
| Any weakness mentioned must be **framed as growth already demonstrated**, or omitted | A genuine caveat left dangling ("er war anfangs unsicher in der Methodik") — German reviewers will act on it |

**The synthesis in one line: German committees do not want superlatives, but they do want a verdict. Give them a verdict backed by counted evidence, and no hedges.** **Classification C, grounded in an A-indirect fact about who reads it.**

## 6. Common weaknesses and how to prevent them

All **Classification C** (convergent across multiple independent Tier 2/3 sources: PrepScholar, Oregon State University National & Global Scholarships Advising, e-fellows, myStipendium, Uni Trier, residencyadvisor), except where noted.

| # | Weakness | Why it kills the letter | Prevention |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | **Generic letter** — could describe any student with a similar CV; no anecdote, no specific interaction | Committees read hundreds; a generic letter signals the referee had nothing to say | Brief the referee with *episodes*, not a CV. Ask for one story told at length (the thesis). |
| 6.2 | **Restating the CV in prose** — "the recogniser turned the bullet points into prose" | Adds zero information over documents the committee already has | Explicitly ask the professor **not** to summarise the CV; the letter's job is what the documents cannot show |
| 6.3 | **Damning with faint praise / lukewarm** — "attentive student, attended regularly, respectful and punctual" = an attendance report, not a recommendation | Reads as the strongest thing the referee could honestly say. Also **fails DAAD's stated requirement of an *unterstützendes* Schreiben (A indirect)** | If the professor cannot be enthusiastic, **change referee**. A signature from a bigger name with less conviction is worth less than conviction. |
| 6.4 | **Too short** | Signals both that the referee had nothing personal to add and that they invested nothing | Ask for a full page; supply enough material that a full page is easy to write |
| 6.5 | **No basis of knowledge stated** | Committee cannot weight the judgement | Section 2 of the structure is non-negotiable |
| 6.6 | **Wrong programme / wrong institution named** | Fatal credibility failure — proves reuse of an old letter | Supply the **exact** string: *MPhil in Industrial Systems, Manufacture and Management, Institute for Manufacturing, Department of Engineering, University of Cambridge*. Proof-read the returned letter before uploading — **and the applicant CAN proof-read it here, because the applicant uploads it (B)** |
| 6.7 | **Unbounded superlatives** | Read as inflation by German committees (Section 5) | Counted comparisons |
| 6.8 | **No mention of the project / study plan** | Leaves DAAD's "Qualität des Vorhabens" criterion unsupported (**B**) | Send the study plan to the referee and ask them to comment on it |
| 6.9 | **Typos, missing signature, missing letterhead, missing Deckblatt** | Formal defect; the Deckblatt is an **A (indirect) requirement** — omitting it makes the application incomplete, and *"unvollständige Bewerbungen werden nicht berücksichtigt"* | Checklist before upload: Deckblatt filled completely + letter + letterhead + date + signature + (stamp) |
| 6.10 | **Stale letter** | DAAD asks for an *"aktuelles"* letter (**A indirect**) | Must be written for this application and dated within the cycle. Do **not** reuse an older Gutachten |
| 6.11 | **Referee misses the deadline** | Application incomplete; and the Deckblatt cannot even be generated after the deadline (**A indirect**) | Ask 4–6 weeks ahead (German university guidance), set an internal deadline ≥1 week before the DAAD deadline, and follow up |

### 7.4 Recommendation

**Primary (B):** the **Bachelor's thesis supervisor**, *provided* they are a professor or hold a doctorate as academic staff — verify this explicitly before asking.

**Fallback (B):** a **module professor who personally assessed substantial individual work**.

**Best available structure if the thesis supervisor is a professor:** thesis supervisor writes the **DAAD Gutachten** (depth of evidence wins with a German committee that wants demonstrable qualification); **Studiengangsleiter or module professor** becomes the **second Cambridge academic referee** (3.12), where institutional framing of the DHBW degree is most useful to an admissions department unfamiliar with it.

> **Do not default to the most senior name.** Every credible source in this research points the same way: evidence and conviction outrank title (6.1, 6.3). **C.**

### 7.5 DHBW-specific risk: the referee is unknown internationally — how to compensate

**Risk (B):** a DAAD committee of German professors will recognise DHBW as a Hochschule but may under-calibrate a dual-university cohort; a Cambridge department may not know DHBW at all. A referee with no international profile provides no reputational shortcut.

**Compensations (all B/C, all cheap):**
1. **Make the comparison group explicit and quantified** — "von 28 Studierenden des Jahrgangs", "die beste von 12 Arbeiten, die ich seit 2022 betreut habe". Converts an unknown reference class into a readable one (3.6).
2. **Have the referee characterise the DHBW dual model in one sentence** — practice-integrated, alternating theory and company phases, i.e. the applicant produced this academic work *while* carrying full-time practice phases. This turns the unfamiliar institution from a discount into a credit. **B.**
3. **Full institutional signature block** — title, chair/Studiengang, DHBW Stuttgart, faculty, contact e-mail and phone, plus an explicit offer to be contacted. Contactability substitutes for fame. **C.**
4. **Institutional letterhead and stamp** (2.4) — stronger signal where the individual is unknown.
5. **Let the evidence carry the weight** — a detailed, falsifiable account of the thesis is legible regardless of the writer's fame; adjectives from an unknown writer are worth nothing. **C.**
6. **For Cambridge specifically**, ensure the second referee adds a different vantage point (institutional/programme-wide) rather than duplicating the first. **B.**

## 8. Draft-letter question: is supplying a draft acceptable?

### 8.1 What the evidence says

**It is common in Germany. That is documented, not speculation.**

- e-fellows.net (Tier 3, but the largest German student/scholarship network and part of this applicant's own network) has an article titled precisely *"Gutachten fürs Stipendium selbst schreiben"* and states that professors asked for a scholarship reference **often say: write it yourself and give it to me to sign**. It then gives operational guidance: heading with applicant name and foundation, information about applicant and referee, **use university/chair letterhead**, the professor typically prints it and makes minor adjustments, and the letter must be error-free with **stamp and signature**.
https://www.e-fellows.net/stipendien/gutachten-selbst-schreiben — **Tier 3 — Classification C**
- myStipendium (Tier 3) similarly advises orienting to the foundation's questions and **producing a "Selbstgutachten"** reflecting one's academic and personal development.
https://www.mystipendium.de/bewerbung-stipendium/empfehlungsschreiben-stipendium — **Tier 3 — C**
- German chairs' own instruction pages routinely require the student to supply motivation letter, CV, full transcript and all formal details **in advance** — i.e. the institutionalised expectation is that the student does the preparatory work, though these pages stop short of asking for a draft.
https://www.old.wiwi.uni-frankfurt.de/abteilungen/finance/lehrstuhl/prof-dr-helmut-gruendl/empfehlungsschreiben.html ; https://www.wiso.uni-hamburg.de/fachbereich-sozoek/professuren/szimayer/service-fuer-studierende/empfehlungsschreiben.html — **Tier 2 — C**
- Counterweight, Tier 2: Uni Trier states a reference *"kann kein bloßes Gefälligkeitsattest sein; die zu bescheinigende besondere Qualifikation muss nachweisbar sein"* — it cannot be a courtesy attestation. The same page tells students to **inform the referee as comprehensively as possible about themselves** and to **disclose whether they have requested references elsewhere**.
https://www.uni-trier.de/universitaet/fachbereiche-faecher/fachbereich-v/personen/professuren/prof-dr-diederich-eckardt/forschung/hinweise-zu-gutachten-und-empfehlungsschreiben — **Tier 2 — C**

**DAAD's own position: NOT VERIFIED.** No DAAD statement was found either permitting or prohibiting applicant-drafted letters. **The portal-generated form may address this — read it (2.3).** Classification of "DAAD permits drafts": **D — unverified, do not assume.**

### 8.2 The risks — and they are asymmetric

| Risk | Severity | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Register collapse** | **High, and the most likely failure** | A self-drafted letter reads like the applicant's motivation letter. A committee reading the motivation letter and the Gutachten back-to-back notices shared vocabulary, rhythm and framing instantly. The two documents sit in the same PDF bundle. |
| **Self-praise paradox** | **High** | Applicants under-claim (embarrassment) or over-claim (over-correction). Both are visible. The one thing a draft *cannot* supply is the referee's independent comparative judgement — which is exactly the part the committee values (3.6). |
| **Loss of the only unique content** | **High** | The letter's whole value is the referee's first-hand observation. A draft written by the applicant, by construction, contains no observation the applicant does not already claim elsewhere → produces exactly weakness 6.2. |
| **Integrity exposure** | **Medium in DE, HIGH in the UK/US** | German practice tolerates the professor delegating drafting because the professor's signature is understood as the act of assessment. **Anglo-American admissions frameworks treat an applicant-written reference as a serious integrity breach.** Cambridge references are submitted confidentially by the referee through a separate portal and are **never disclosed to the student** (3.12) — the entire design presumes the student neither writes nor reads them. |
| **Cross-contamination between the two applications** | **HIGH — this applicant's specific danger** | The same professor will plausibly write both the DAAD Gutachten and a Cambridge reference. If the professor has been handed a draft for DAAD, the temptation to recycle it into the Cambridge Referee Portal is obvious. **That would put applicant-authored text into a confidential UK academic reference.** This is the single most serious risk identified in this research. |
| **Professor's reputational exposure** | Medium | If discovered, the referee is compromised; a referee who refuses to be put in that position is behaving correctly. |

### 8.3 Verdict and recommendation

> ### **Recommendation: DO NOT offer a draft letter. Offer a BRIEFING instead. Classification C.**
>
> **If, and only if, the professor explicitly and unprompted asks for a draft** (which German sources say is common), the position is:
> - **Acceptable in the German context for the DAAD Gutachten** (C), *provided* the professor reads, edits, adds their own comparative judgement, and signs on letterhead — i.e. the professor performs the assessment and owns the text.
> - **Never for the Cambridge reference.** If the professor asks for a draft for Cambridge, decline politely and supply the briefing instead. **State explicitly to the professor that the Cambridge reference must be their own text submitted directly through Cambridge's Referee Portal** (3.12). **B, grounded in Cambridge's A-indirect confidentiality rule.**
> - If a DAAD draft is written, **the applicant must deliberately break register**: different sentence structure, different vocabulary from the motivation letter, third person throughout, German academic letter form, and **leave the comparative/ranking sentence blank for the professor to fill in** — the applicant cannot honestly write "among the best students I have taught".
>
> **The better play, and the one to lead with:** the briefing pack in Section 9. It gets 90% of the benefit of a draft (the professor has everything, writing is quick, nothing is forgotten, the right programme is named) with none of the register, integrity or cross-contamination risk. Most German guidance on what to send a referee describes exactly this: purpose, institution, deadlines, formalities, motivation letter, CV, transcript.
> Sources: https://www.uni-trier.de/... ; https://www.old.wiwi.uni-frankfurt.de/... ; https://www.wiso.uni-hamburg.de/... — **Tier 2 — C**

## 10. Contradictions and unverified items

### Contradictions

**C1 — Portal upload vs. sealed postal submission.**
Current DAAD programme pages for *Stipendien für ein Masterstudium im Ausland* and sibling programmes state the applicant uploads both documents in the portal and that **e-mail submission is not provided for** (no mention of post). DAAD's older *FAQs für Deutsche* (marked **"Stand: 18. Juni 2015"**) and some DAAD general pages describe recommendation letters being sent **in a sealed envelope by post to DAAD, Kennedyallee 50, 53175 Bonn**, possibly by the referee directly, with the **postmark decisive**; and general DAAD guidance states that *"Bewerbungen … werden ausschließlich über das DAAD-Portal eingereicht, mit Ausnahme des Gutachtens."*
**Assessment:** the postal/sealed route is **legacy or programme-specific** (it clearly applies to some other DAAD programmes). **For this programme, follow the current programme page: portal upload.** **Verify in the portal, which is authoritative for the live cycle.**
Sources: https://www2.daad.de/ausland/studieren/stipendium/de/70-stipendien-finden-und-bewerben/?detail=57503584 vs. https://www2.daad.de/medien/ausland/dokumente/faq_f%C3%BCr_deutsche.pdf and https://www.daad.de/de/im-ausland-studieren-forschen-lehren/stipendien-finanzierung/bewerbung/

**C2 — "Hochschullehrkraft" vs. the extension to doctorate-holding academic staff.**
The programme page names a *Hochschullehrkraft*; DAAD's general guidance extends eligibility to *promovierte wissenschaftliche Mitarbeitende* **for this named programme**. Not a true contradiction, but the programme page alone would mislead. **Use the extension, and keep the source to hand in case of query.**

**C3 — Register: German understatement vs. DAAD's demand for an *unterstützendes* letter.**
Resolved in Section 5: German *form*, unambiguous *verdict*.
